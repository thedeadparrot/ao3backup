You knew it was your cavalier by the footsteps on the floor. His boots had a familiar rhythm to them. No one else would look for you in the facility rooms.

"You should be asleep," he said. His forehead furrowed with concern.

You thought, very briefly, of smoothing out the wrinkles with your fingertips, either with the touch of your hands or the simple application of flesh magic. You suspected he wouldn't appreciate it. Over the course of your two-year partnership, you'd learned that he disliked what he termed to be "frivolous" uses of necromancy, especially when it came to his own body.

"I'm close to figuring it out," you said to him. It _was_ late, and you would expect to see him dressed down, ready for sleep. Instead, he was still in his formal Fourth House blues, rapier at his side. Even if the dark bags underneath his eyes were deeper than your own, he was still handsome. He was always handsome.

You were never meant to be a necromancer. While the Fourth House was always looking for necromancers and cavaliers, you were small and quiet. It had never presented to you the way it had for your classmates. The thanergy of the Nine Houses was never screamed to you to wield it. Growing up, you were trained to be a cohort soldier. You learned how to position on a battlefield, how to smash through enemy defenses. You were told it was an honor to be part of the Emperor's vanguard, the tip of his spear.

On your first tour of duty, you tasted your first death. It burst on your tongue as you watched the enemy combatant draw their last breath. The flavor was sweet, like the candies you were only allowed to have on high holidays. You rolled the thanergy around in your mouth, savoring it for as long as you could, and then you used it to rip the skull out of someone's head. You were thirteen at the time. When you returned to your commander, you told her what had happened. She asked you to prove it, and after you managed to demonstrate rudimentary thalergy transfer after a few attempts, they gave you a cavalier.

But that was years ago. In the present, on the First House, your cavalier said, "You'll be closer after a good night of sleep. The theorems won't be any different in the morning." He was your second cavalier. Your first had also been tall and lanky, also had a kind smile. He had stuck by your side every day, looked after you while you ate and slept and desperately tried to catch up on your studies, since you were far behind anyone else your age. You would stay up late until your eyes would water, and the letters would start to swim. Your first cavalier -- Nathan, his name was Nathan -- would shake his head in the morning and hand you cups of coffee. He would do things for you before you even asked for them. Notes neatly organized. Equipment cleaned until it shone. In battle, he would forge ahead of you, fearless and calm. No one had ever made you feel that safe.

You had stayed in that holding pattern for years, right until Nathan was caught in the blast radius of a thanergetic fission reaction while you had been deployed on a colony planet whose name you couldn't even remember anymore.

That thanergy bloom had tasted sour, had left you nearly blind and sobbing with rage. And when you returned to the barracks, you were covered in blood and entrails with only the vaguest memories of what happened after Nathan's death. By then, you'd made it to the ripe old age of eighteen.

Your captain had given you a new cavalier right then and there, one who had also lost an adept in the battle. One with big, tired eyes and a deep gash on his left arm. It had stopped bleeding, but his uniform was still stained brown around the wound. He'd stuck out his hand, and you'd shaken it. It wasn't like either of you could get any dirtier. "Wout," he had said. And that was that.

You looked over the notes you'd scribbled onto the flimsy. There was something you were missing. You could see the shape of it. Soul manipulation. Energy transfer. You'd spent weeks with the trials, and it didn't feel like you were any closer to wrapping your head around it. You licked your lips. "All right," you said, conceding.

When you got up onto your feet again, your head swam. Wout reached out to support your back, one large palm pressed against your spine. "Careful," he said.

You followed Wout back up the ladder, back towards your rooms. You knew the Eighth House was closing to cracking the secret as well. Their adept was a smiling boy even younger than you who always seemed to be a step ahead in the process. In theory, it wasn't a competition -- all of you could become Lyctors if you passed the trials. But since you'd set foot on the First House, since the priests had destroyed your shuttles, every house was wary and on high alert and mistrustful of each other, you included.

From the day they'd declared you the representative of the Fourth House for the Lyctor trials, you had known that you needed to win this. There could be no greater honor, no better way to serve the glorious Emperor, the King Undying, the Necrolord Prime. And the Fourth House was nothing without its honor.

You didn't pass anyone else in the hallways. Not even the grim-faced, curly-haired adept of the Second, who treated everyone else like children, nor the big-eared cavalier who always haunted his steps. You were especially relieved to avoid the mismatched pair from the Third. They always tried to show a united front, even though it was clear to everyone, even Teacher, they were at odds. You felt certain that one day, you'd find the remains of the blond necromancer in one of the testing chambers after her cavalier left her to die alone in it.

Wout pushed open the door to your rooms and ushered you inside. Compared to Cohort barracks or a ship's quarters they were large and generous, which meant Wout had his own rooms. They were adjoining. Since he'd grown up as Fourth as you had and was used to cramped, shared spaces, he spent more time in your quarters than not. Maybe in a different life, you would prefer solitude, but in this one, you wanted the company.

The rooms were as you left them. That wasn't much of a surprise. You both packed light, and Wout kept the few meager keys you'd managed to collect on his person. You still let out a sigh of relief to see the familiar site of your traveller's trunk, still left open with clothes spilling out. The stack of disorganized flimsies on the side table. After a life lived in military precision, you appreciated the chance to loosen up. Wout took the opposite tack. He kept his rooms as neat as if you were still on the front lines. The corners of his bedsheets were always immaculate.

Wout shrugged off his coat, then undid the top few buttons of his shirt and rolled up his sleeves. The movement revealed the red lines of scars, which you knew were far more extensive than what showed right now. Some of them were the natural result of surviving through battles. Others were the carefully drawn sigils of protection wards. You'd carved them onto his arms and back and legs yourself, pressed the knife into his skin as you sealed thanergy into each one, prayed that this would be the one that would keep him alive, keep him safe.

"You haven't eaten in a while," he said. Your stomach did ache a bit, but only a distant sort of way, buried underneath the exhaustion. It might reassert itself in the morning. You could deal with it then.

You said, "It's fine. Let's just-- we should get some sleep," because it distracted you from how much you wanted to touch him.

He smiled at you. A soft smile. A tired smile. His eyes were large and dark. You'd always liked the rich brown of his eyes. They were warm, in the way Wout was warm, and they were solid, in the way Wout was solid. It was understood that every necromancer needed a cavalier. But what you felt for Wout did not fit neatly into that categorization. It was like what you felt for Nathan and yet completely different. It was too much, too big. Wout gave your shoulder a pat with one large hand. "Yeah," he said.

You felt him pull away, and you felt-- you couldn't---

Nathan had liked to stay close. Wout liked to venture out and risk his life to harvest fresh kills from the battlefield. Maybe that's why you had barely let him out of your sight since you landed at Canaan House. Maybe that's why you always wanted him to be closer. There were so many things that wanted to take the things you loved away from you. So many were already gone. You didn't want Wout to be another one.

"Wait," you blurted out.

He paused, then turned back to you. He raised his eyebrows in an unspoken question.

This would be crossing a line, you knew. This wouldn't be something that could be written off as necessity, a matter of convenience. But you were trapped on the First House, with only the other Houses and some aging priests for company. And you were tired, oh so tired. "Stay?" you asked.

Wout studied you for a moment. You felt your neck heat. But you also didn't waver. You met his gaze and didn't flinch. "You are close, aren't you?" he asked.

You let out a deep sigh and shrugged. "There's just one last thing--" Every time you thought you got closer, it seemed to slip further out of sight.

Wout gave you a gentle, reassuring smile. "I know you'll figure it out. Maybe even tonight." He reached into his coat, which was draped over the back of a chair, and pulled out a flask. You watched as he unscrewed the top and downed the whole thing in one go. While the priests of the First House didn't provide alcohol, you were sure that the Fifth House had smuggled in their own stash and was trading it for favors. Then he slid it back into its hidden pocket and said, "Okay."

You blinked in surprise. You had been so certain he'd put up a fight. Then again, you weren't asking for anything unusual, nothing as base as sex. He just sat down on the bed and unlaced his shoes. You shed your own outer layers and stifled a yawn.

When you crawled into bed, Wout was already there. The bed was a moderate size. Compared to your usual military cots, it felt like a luxury. You curled up against his side. There was so much of him. You felt surrounded, enveloped. His scent was everywhere. It filled your nostrils. This was the closest you'd ever been to each other, physically. If you pressed a hand to his chest, you wondered if you could reach inside him.

One of his hands settled on your back. He held weapons in those hands -- a rapier, a knife, a broadsword. He had killed people with those hands. You had seen them stained red with blood and brown with dirt. You knew them intimately, the long, square fingers and the dark hair on the knuckles and the callouses on the palms. You had learned the map of his body with your necromancy. You had mended broken bones and relocated slipped joints. Could you search even deeper? Find something underneath the flesh and blood of his physical body?

The tests in the labs underneath the house were about this. You let your hand settle on his chest. His heart beat thrummed in your own chest. Your eyes fell closed. You reached out with your necromancy, looking for-- yes, there it was.

You didn't know how to describe a soul. Perhaps if you were a poet of the Fifth House, you could find the words. Wout's soul wasn't a ball of glowing light. It wasn't soft or warm. It was just a soul.

But also, it was _Wout's_ soul. You clung to it, too tired and too sleepy to be careful. He was a deep, vast well of thalergy. You wanted to sip from it, just a little. Not the greedy slurps you'd taken during the avulsion trial. You always wanted more of him. You would take anything he would give you, and he was willing to give up so much. Maybe everything.

Wout squeezed your shoulder. "Sleep," he said. His voice rumbled in his chest. Not quite a suggestion and not quite a command either.

You could protest or resist, but your tired mind had no defenses left. You slept.

* * *
When you woke up again, Wout was still there. Your nose was jammed into the crease of his elbow, and your ear had shoved itself into Wout's armpit sometime in the night.

And there, still nestled in the crook of your necromancy, was Wout's soul. It hadn't shifted out of your grasp in the middle of the night. Your pulse kicked up. This is what you'd been missing from the mega theorem. How to fix the soul in place and keep it from deteriorating. You had seen the equations, but you hadn't been able to put it all together, put the theory into practice, until your half-asleep mind had reached for Wout's soul. You supposed that made sense. All the previous tests required you to work together as a pair.

You pushed yourself up to a sitting position, your body jolted awake as your mind finished untangling the puzzle. Wout stirred next to you. He rolled over, curling himself around the space where you were.

His face was still slack with sleep, month parted, drool collecting on the pillow below. He looked soft and vulnerable, and you drank the sight in. You wanted to commit it to memory. You never wanted to lose it.

Then Wout started coughing. The sound was awful, deep heaving sounds that wracked his chest and seemed to vibrate through his whole body. Something was wrong. You knew it.

In a panic, you reached out. You could feel the unsteady thrum of Wout's thalergy, disrupted somehow. He coughed again. This time, the coughing fit was bad enough, red splatters of blood landed on the sheets.

You clambered over, untangling yourself from the blankets just enough you could straddle his lap. He had been fine last night. You had been so close last night. You would have been able to *tell*. You let go of the soul for now, so you could try to figure out what was wrong with his body.

It was poison, you realized. Something slow-acting enough a necromancer wouldn't be able to pick up on it until it was too late. Your first instinct was to say it was the Seventh House. The cavalier of that House had some sort of grudge against Wout that you didn't understand. Earlier, you could have drawn it out before it hit his bloodstream, but now it was everywhere, saturated through his entire system.

Wout stared up at you. His chest wracked with another coughing fit, but his eyes were calm. "You figured it out. The last step in the theorem," he choked out.

"You're *dying*," you snarled back. You couldn't believe he wanted to talk about the stupid theorem while his thalergy was bleeding out. You could feel the beginnings of the thanergy bloom. Your hands scrabbled on his chest. Maybe you could slow it down, give you both a few precious minutes. You did what you could. You didn't have the expertise of the Sixth House and their medical arts.

"I know," he said. "You knew, too. What it would take." Another cough. Another splatter of blood.

The realization began to dawn on you. "You-- what you drank last night--"

"One flesh, one end," he said. The mantra of the necromancer and the cavalier. He must have repeated it a million times, and now you understood how much he believed in it. Enough to take it to this final conclusion. "I knew before we got here. Not the theorems or the--" More coughing. "-- but the math was simple."

"One flesh, one end," you answered back. How many times had you repeated that phrase? To him and Nathan both. You had lost count. You wanted to rage against the world. For ripping another cavalier away from you. But this wasn't the world, not really. This was all *Wout*

Wout grinned up at you, wide enough to show you his blood-stained teeth. "Couldn't let you drag your feet," he said. "Needed to make sure we won."

If he had told you-- if he had let you in on his plan-- maybe you could have made this decision together, maybe you could have-- You didn't really believe that. Wout could be stubborn. Even if he could be swayed by reason, Wout liked to follow through on his plans.

You leaned forward. You cupped his face in your hands and found his soul with your necromancy. His eyes fell closed as you kissed him. You had never kissed him before, and all the reasons for that felt small, insignificant in the face of these revelations.

The harsh, metallic taste of blood flooded your mouth. You swallowed it. You had to take this piece of him inside you. A soul will always long for its body. You had to take that body into yourself before--

You hated him for making you do this.

You were also maybe a little bit grateful for it. 

You committed this to memory, too. The wet heat of his mouth. The scratchy prickle of his morning stubble. His lips parted. His body was a solid weight beneath yours. He opened up for you, gave up everything. It was awful and beautiful all at once. You grabbed hold of his soul again. You couldn't let it slip away, not while you were so close.

If you screwed this up, he would be gone for good. Gone forever. Just like Nathan.

You bit down on the full bottom lip that had been taunting you for *years*. If you were going to be forced into this, if Wout was going to rip this choice from you, you would take this for yourself. Your bite might have drawn blood. You couldn't tell. You bit harder, trying to get just that little bit deeper into him.

He let out another cough. His breath wheezed into your mouth. His skin had taken on a green tinge. With all of his organs shutting down, this would be an ugly death. Wout deserved better than an ugly death.

You undid your wards, stripping them from him one by one. The red scars faded into a dull white. You had put them there, and it felt right to be the one taking them off him. Then, when he was completely bare, completely vulnerable before you, you found the racing, unsteady beat of his heart.

And you stopped it.

The resulting thanergy bloom nearly blinded you. You seized as much of it as you could. You needed to keep hold of Wout's soul before it could disappear into the River, that place where souls went after death. You needed to keep it here with you.

You pulled it into your waiting month. The thanergy tasted awful, that same sour-sick flavor of Nathan's death. You reminded yourself that this one was the last one. You would never have to experience this again. You would have Wout with you always, from now until the end of time. You would be a Lyctor. With Wout's soul powering your necromancy, you would never be alone again.

You opened your mouth wider, and you swallowed Wout's soul whole.

* * *

There was more to do after that. You had to keep the soul within yourself, preserve it, tether it to the parts of Wout you had consumed, then make sure you could draw on the thanergetic power for your necromancy. It was so much power, more power than you had ever felt before in your entire life. You understood the theory of it. A soul leaving the body in the moment of death was one of the most potent sources of thanergy in the universe. But in most cases, it only lasted for a second, brief blip and then gone. This soul, your cavalier's soul, Wout's soul, would be trapped there for the rest of eternity.

You didn't move Wout's body. You left it lying on the bed, but you did use your newfound power to pull the poison from his system and prevent the natural cycle of decay. You even brought back a tinge of pink to his skin. You wanted to remember him like this, to remember this as the first and last time you had kissed him. He used to joke about making sure to use his body to make the biggest explosion when he died. He told you he wanted you to make him a thangery blast so powerful no one would ever forget seeing or feeling it. It had just been battlefield humor. You hadn't let yourself ever consider his death.

A lock of hair had fallen over his forehead. You brushed it away with one hand. You lingered for longer than you should have. His skin was cool and soft to the touch. Wout had taken so much from you. It felt fair that you could keep this much of him in return. You couldn't resist the urge to drop a kiss to his lifeless forehead, to smooth away the lingering dark bags underneath his eyes. You thought maybe he would understand.

You looked down to see Wout's blank face. The blood on his lips had dried into a rusty brown. A dribble of blood had run down his chin. You didn't like the look of it, so you licked it clean, dragging your tongue over his chilly skin. You wanted to taste him again, even if this version was a faded imitation of the oxygen-rich blood pumped through a living heart.

A deep, awful ache tried to chew its way from your stomach into your chest as you did so. You would never hear his voice again, you realized. You could make his face form that familiar smile with its familiar crinkles at the corners of his eyes, but some part of him would always be missing. Even with your new power, you wouldn't be able to bring the life back into his eyes. You shoved the feeling down, shoved it away, even as tears began to well up in the corners of your eyes.

There was no place for crying in a war zone, you reminded yourself. You were a soldier, first and foremost. You had done what you needed to do. You straightened up, turning away, wiping your eyes and face clean. You had cleaned up Wout for now. Now it was time to look towards the next thing.

You were still in yesterday's clothes, and your mouth was still smeared with Wout's blood. You would need to clean yourself up before you could face the other Houses. Perhaps being a Lyctor now meant you could show up however you wanted, but House decorum hadn't vanished the second the transformation happened. You went into the restroom you'd shared with Wout. His toothbrush was still sitting on the counter. Something else to deal with later. As you ran a wet towel over your lips, you caught sight of yourself in the mirror.

For the most part, you looked the same today as you did yesterday, except your hair was mussed and--

You blinked once, twice. Deep brown eyes looked back at you in the mirror. They were Wout's eyes, not your own blue ones. You stared at your reflection, considering the change and adapting your mental image of yourself to accommodate it. You liked it, you decided after a minute. You would have missed seeing Wout's eyes. Now, with his soul burning in your chest, you wouldn't have to.
