"Come here," Wout said. His voice was low, raspy after a long day of riding hard, crying, and giving interviews and speeches. "Please."

Christophe decided this was his favorite version of Wout: beaming from ear-to-ear, his eyes half-lidded from the champagne, his hair tousled and falling over his forehead. The soft hotel lighting cast shadows along his cheekbones and jaw. Christophe hesitated. He was reluctant to disrupt the perfection of this picture, this moment of Wout still radiant with pure joy. But then again, a moment was just that-- a moment.

They would get up tomorrow, and the world would move on. They would get assigned their next training plans and turn their preparation towards the Tour. Their next races were just around the corner. Christophe had always felt like lingering for too long on the good was just as dangerous as getting mired in the bad. 

And yet-- the cobblestone trophy sat on the bedside table. Larger, somehow, than those times he had watched the podium ceremony on television. Solid proof that this wasn't just an elaborate dream.

Christophe reached over to cup Wout's cheek with one hand and he leaned in so he could kiss him the way he'd wanted to earlier in front of all the cameras, when Wout would taste like the dust and sweat and grime of the pavé. He couldn't at the time, not with so many eyes on them. Instead, he had knelt down and wrapped Wout up in his arms and let Wout curl into him and sob brokenly into his shoulder, all the relief and exhaustion pouring out of him at once. He had told Wout he was incredible, that he was proud of him, but he wasn't sure Wout was hearing much of anything at that point.

Now, like he had before, Wout wrapped a large hand around the back of Christophe's neck. His hand was steady this time, without the tremors that had shook him earlier. His smile against Christophe's lips hadn't faded one watt. Christophe could almost taste the joy spilling from him. It had notes of the mayonnaise and salt and potatoes from the Belgian frites they'd had for dinner.

Wout swayed closer, leaning into Christophe so that Christophe had to take some of his weight. Christophe tightened his grip in response. He wanted Wout pressed against him. He wanted to feel the reality of Wout's classics-honed body against his own.

Christophe was tired, almost dead on his feet. Roubaix and its cobbles took their toll on everyone. Christophe felt like he could sleep for a week, but he was still seized by the same desire he felt at the end of the race: to hold Wout in his joy and his relief and know he had been part of it.

"You were so strong today," Wout murmured into Christophe's mouth.

The words left a shivery trail of pleasure running down Christophe's spine. It felt wrong-- like the world turning upside down, because Wout was the one who won today. He was the one who split the group on the Arenberg. He was the one who made the attack that pulled both him and Pogačar away from their reduced group. He was the one who won the sprint in the velodrome.

All Christophe had to do was sit on and make everyone else's life more difficult while he got periodic updates from Grischa on the radio.

Christophe pulled back. "Today is about you," he said. "You don't have to--" He bit back the rest of his words. He knew Wout. Wout only ever did what he wanted to do.

Wout pressed his forehead to Christophe's, keeping their faces close like he didn't want to be apart either. "You were there for me when I needed you," he said. "I missed you last year." He let the statement hang there, his voice heavy with emotion. Christophe had watched the race while camped out on his couch. He wasn't capable of doing much more than that at the time, so he had been glued to the television, watching Wout fall out of position and then claw his way back to a fourth place finish anyway. He had felt sick with longing at the sight of it, just as he had felt sick with longing the year before, when Wout had missed the race because of his injuries.

Christophe let out a sigh. "You have no idea how much I wanted to be there." He knew what this race meant to Wout. He had learned it in his first year, when Nathan had pulled him aside to give him the whole explanation. Christophe had even ridden in Wout's first Roubaix. He remembered the story of Michael Goolaerts back when it had happened. But for him, the news of a rider dying during the race had been shocking and sad, but it hadn't been *personal* in the way it had been for Wout.

For years, Christophe had known he had to win this race for Wout. It was why he had been hired, yes, but as he acclimated to the team and got to know Wout better as a person and a friend --- and then more than that --- the professional became personal for him, too.

Wout said, "You were there after Arenberg today. That's more important." His fingers tightened on Christophe's neck, the blunt tips digging into skin. "Everything was chaos. I had no idea where you were during that whole sector. I just had to-- I had to focus on my line and keep riding. But then we were clear, and you were still with me."

It hadn't been easy. Christophe had to claw his way up through the smashed remnants of the peloton, past all the punctures and mechanicals, and he was still trailing behind Bisseger as they rolled off the cobbles. But he had seen Wout's silver helmet up ahead. He dug deep, because-- "I knew you needed me up there," Christophe said.

Three years ago, Christophe had survived the decisive split before puncturing and falling out of it. He and Nathan had been together after that point, chasing the rest of the way to the velodrome. Neither of them could help after Wout's final, fatal puncture.

And then Christophe had been forced to pick up the pieces in the aftermath. Wout had a tendency to analyze things to death. Once an idea got stuck in his head, it required a lot of effort on Christophe's part to dislodge it. Pleasant effort overall, but effort all the same.

Still, Christophe had been determined not to repeat the experience this year.

Wout let out a deep sigh. "I couldn't have done it without you. You were perfect." His words had become a bit fuzzy at the edges, but his eyes were still bright and alert.

Christophe moved his hand upwards so that he could drag his fingers through Wout's hair. It was a good enough distraction from the warmth pooling in his belly. Even if the mind was willing, his body wasn't.

Wout tilted his head back so that Christophe could get a better angle. A soft smile graced his lips. Wout was always hungry for touch. A hand on his back or on his shoulder. A hug after a race, win or lose. And then, when they were alone, curling up together, skin pressed against skin.

"Do you want to take a shower?" Christophe asked. He knew Wout had already scrubbed himself down in the team bus and changed into a fresh jersey for the podium ceremony after the end of the women's race. He didn't seem inclined to use the traditional velodrome showers despite some of the inquiries from nearby photographers.

Christophe didn't blame him. After a win like that, everyone would want a piece of him, and they'd already gotten more than their fair share already. Wout already had to live so much of his life in the public eye. He deserved to keep some things for himself.

Wout nodded, "Yeah," he said. "Haven't really gotten a chance to wash my hair yet." He settled his weight more fully into Christophe's shoulder.

Christophe led them both towards the small hotel bathroom. Wout lazily shed his t-shirt and shoved off his sweatpants and boxers. Christophe, who was wearing the same team-branded gear, did likewise.

He had seen Wout naked countless times before, but the sight never got old. Lean arms. Thick thighs.  Deepening tan lines. They weren't as dark or as stark as they would get during the long July days of the Tour but they were unmistakably those of a cyclist. The classics season had whittled him down until he was mostly skin and muscle and bone.

Christophe placed his hands on Wout's shoulders, ostensibly to guide him towards the shower, but also so he could trace a finger over one of the new scars on Wout's collarbone. That one, Christophe knew, had been acquired at DDV.

Over the years, Christophe had made a complete inventory, cataloguing each one with the care of a dedicated researcher. Wout had gained so many new ones since their last Paris-Roubaix together. The deep gash on his right knee from the Vuelta. The surgery on his foot after he broke it during his CX season. All of them times when Christophe wasn't there to look out for him.

He fiddled with the shower controls, getting the water temperature nice and hot. Christophe wasn't particularly picky about it, but Wout liked his showers scalding unless it was a sweltering summer. Once it was at a temperature that Wout would tolerate, Christophe nudged Wout inside.

This wasn't the largest or the most comfortable shower Christophe had ever been in. Its small size forced them close together, with Christophe's front sliding up against Wout's back. Wout had already been relaxed all evening, but as the water poured down onto him, some extra layer of stress seemed to melt away from him. He tilted his face up towards the spray with his eyes closed.

When Wout reached up to run his fingers through his hair, Christophe knocked his hands away. "Let me," he said.

Wout's earlier shower had rinsed away most of the dust and dirt from the race. Christophe wanted to wash the rest of it clean. In the depth of the spring classics, Christophe always felt the most protective of Wout, and not just because it was a specialty for both of them. The feeling was probably a side effect of racing so much in Belgium and seeing all the attention Wout drew, both positive and negative. Wout was strong, but the constant pressure still left its marks on him.

Christophe reached over to grab the bottle of shampoo and lathered up his hands. He could keep doing this for Wout even if the race was over. Wout sighed when Christophe ran his fingers through his hair, Christophe always felt deeply honored to have Wout's trust and his faith. Not just anyone was allowed to care for him this way.

As Christophe worked, he could feel Wout's shoulders start to shake. All the emotion of the day was bubbling up again. Wout had cried in front of the crowd and the cameras without any trace of self-consciousness. And yet, Christophe knew that this one was personal and private.

He didn't draw attention to Wout's sobs, just focused on rinsing the shampoo thoroughly from Wout's hair. If Wout wanted to talk, he would talk, and until then, Christophe would make sure his hair was clean. The shower was filled with steam. The splashes of water were almost hot enough to scald.

Christophe found the soap next, worked his hands over Wout's back, where he wouldn't have been able to reach himself in the term shower. There were also scars on Wout's back: pale, raised lines crossing over the length of his shoulder blades and down his ribs. Christophe let his fingers linger on each of them in turn, updating his mental catalogue.

Eventually, Wout's breathing evened out again. "Thank you," he murmured. He turned around so that the spray was at his back and his dark brown eyes met Christophe's. He said, "I didn't want-- It's one thing to have photographers in my face all the time out there. But then I couldn't-- when--" His voice trailed off.

"You don't have to explain anything," Christophe said. He cupped Wout's face and kissed him again. Wout had already been interviewed in a variety of languages today. He had peeled himself open enough times. Christophe wasn't going to demand it from him, too.

Wout huffed out a soft laugh into Christophe's mouth. He placed his hands on Christophe's shoulders and swapped their positions so that Christophe's back was now under the shower's spray. "Your turn," Wout said.

Christophe wanted to protest. This was Wout's day. And besides, Christophe hadn't had the same media obligations and then a podium ceremony, which meant he could take a downright indulgent shower earlier.

But this was as much a part of Wout as anything else. Maybe even more so. He could ride as hard for his teammates as he could ride for himself and oftentimes harder. He ran his hands carefully over Christophe's neck, Christophe's arms, Christophe's chest. His blunt fingernails dragged over Christophe's nipples and through his sparse, wiry hair, leaving a warm, shivery sensation in their wake.

After Wout was done, Christophe reached behind himself to shut off the water. The steam still sat heavy and humid in the room. Wout got to the towels first. He grabbed one and wrapped Christophe up in it. This hotel wasn't fancy enough to have particularly luxurious towels, but it still felt nice to be wrapped up in one.

Wout ran his own towel over his hair, leaving it mussed and messy. He was smiling now. His expression was suffused with softness. The sight of him swamped Christophe with a wave of affection. When he first joined the team, Wout intimidated him. He was so strong, so handsome, so fearsome in the bunch. It hadn't taken long to learn this side of him: the silly, the gentle, the sweet. No longer a figure to be revered, but a man who could be loved.

They didn't bother getting dressed before crawling into bed. Even if sex was off the table, it still felt important to feel skin against skin with no barriers between them. Wout slid in behind Christophe and wrapped his arms around him. Christophe said, "In my interviews, I said you deserved to win this race a thousand times over."

He couldn't see Wout's face, but he could hear a slight hitch in his breathing. Wout's voice was raspy when he responded. "Thank you," he said. His arms tightened around Christophe's waist. His body was so warm and so alive. "I'm glad you never stopped believing in me, even when I-- It means--" He seemed to lose his words again, but it didn't matter.

Christophe lifted one of Wout's hands from its grip around his midsection so he could hold it in his own.

All those years, all those kilometers together. Christophe had been so nervous leaving Cofidis, to join a new team with new expectations. He knew he was going to work for Wout, to win him races in general and one of the cobbled monuments in particular. It had seemed inevitable at the time, but life had its own way of twisting and turning. And now after five years, a lifetime, a blink of an eye, it had finally happened.

Christophe felt so grateful he had been here to witness it and to be part of it. He pressed a soft kiss to Wout's palm. "I love you too," he said.
