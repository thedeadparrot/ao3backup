Christophe smiles into the kiss. "Agree to disagree," he murmurs back.

---

"I thought we saw enough of the mountain yesterday," Christophe gripes as Wout leads them up the first set of stairs.

Their lazy morning in means they don't have a massive amount of time before they have to be at the airport again, so Wout proposed climbing up to the lookout point of Mont Royal, following the Grand Staircase instead of the winding road their route had followed the day before. Christophe was willing to go along with it, but he wasn't exactly enthusiastic about the idea.

The weather is mild. Some of the early morning clouds have cleared way for patches of sunlight. A good day to be outside.

"The view is supposed to be pretty," Wout says. This particular route is certainly popular enough. The stairs are crowded with tourists, one side going up, the other headed down. Wout grabs hold of Christophe's hand, so they won't get separated.

Because the stairs cut up through the forest, the path is wooded and shady. It looks like a different world from the brutal course a day before. The atmosphere is peaceful despite the footsteps and chatter of the tourists who fill the staircase. Compared to the grinding of brakes and clacking of gears and the roar of the watching crowd, this feels almost silent. Christophe continues to grumble that they could have stayed in bed, but he doesn't resist as Wout leads them ever upwards.

Being out here reminds Wout of some of the off-road trails he rides for training. A slice of nature separate from the modern world. A place to get some distance and perspective that can be difficult to find when he's surrounded by all the anxieties of his life and his job. As they climb higher and higher, he can feel himself shedding his worries bit by bit. He breathes and breathes out, and he lets it all go.

A few of the tourists in front of them are panting with exertion when they reach the top. Wout just feels invigorated from getting in some exercise. Despite his complaints, Christophe looks happy, too. They're in a period of sun, and as they emerge from the trees, the sunlight highlights his tanned skin.

That tan was hard won from months of working himself back into race form. Wout likes what it represents as much as he likes the glow of Christophe's skin. Christophe smiles the un-self-concious smile that shows off all his gums and teeth. That smile has been a rare sight over the last year.

From the viewing platform, the city of Montreal spreads out before them. "It is a nice view," Christophe concedes. One of the benefits of being a pro cyclist is getting to travel the world. And though the schedule can be harsh and demanding, there's still time for them to carve out moments of beauty and joy.

They stand there in silence for a while. Below them, the city hustles and bustles its way through its Monday routine. Around them, the tourists stream in and out of the lookout point. They pose for group photos and selfies as they chatter in a variety of languages. Wout shoots a few pics himself: a couple of the view, which he might add to his weekly photo dump later, and a candid of Christophe with his hands resting on the stone wall of the lookout point. Even with his eyes hidden underneath his sunglasses, his expression is soft and content.

Wout knows he won't be posting that photograph anywhere. This is a memory he is going to keep for himself.

---

At Wout's insistence (it's still his birthday, after all, and he gets to do what he wants), they take a different way back down the mountain. During their recon before the race, he caught sight of a mural at the edge of the park. He wants to get closer to get another look at it before they leave.

Christophe claims he didn't really notice it despite the number of laps they did, which only fuels Wout's desire to see it again.

The mural is visible from a distance as they approach that corner of the park. It's huge and colorful, depicting a cyclist celebrating a win, one of his hands raised to the sky in victory. The older part of the city on this side of the park is full of murals. They caught sight of a few of them during their evening ride last night. Most of them have more of a grungy, indie aesthetic and are closer to ground level. This one is high up enough it can be seen over the neighboring rooftops.

They get closer. In the upper right corner of the mural, smaller words explain that it was painted in honor of Hugo Houle for being the first Quebecois winner of a stage in the Tour de France in 2022.

Wout vaguely remembers that stage. It had been a breakaway win in the Pyrenees. Wout had been with the team in the peloton, working to limit attacks on Jonas, when they announced the win over the radio.

Christophe says, "That was a good Tour."

It had been an amazing Tour for the whole team. Wout in green with three stage wins. Jonas taking home the yellow and the polka dots. Christophe sneaking across the line for his first and only stage win so far. "Yeah, it was," Wout agrees.

Christophe glances over at him with a thoughtful expression on his face. "Do you wish we could go back?" he asks.

Wout lets the question sit inside his head and marinate for a bit. "No," he says eventually. It could never happen. He likes to focus on the things he can control and change, not things he can't. And also--

In 2022, Wout had been coming off an ugly breakup for the Tour. His wins that year had been driven as much by hurt and spite as by ambition. He had been crushing hard on Christophe back then, had even considered propositioning him at one point. They were all used to being naked around each other and even the cameras, but sometimes Christophe's eyes lingered a second longer than was acceptable. Wout himself had spent years perfecting the rules of straight boy behavior, and he could spot deviations from kilometers away. But then Christophe himself found a new girlfriend after the Tour, and the whole point was moot.

Christophe says, "I don't think so either." He presses a kiss to Wout's cheek. "But I would like to win something again." He lets out a small sigh.

"You will," Wout promises. "I did, after all." The corner of his mouth ticks up. He understands Christophe's frustration on a visceral level. He was there this spring, wondering if he was always going to feel this weak, this incapable of matching the rest of the peloton.

Christophe's answering smile is small and thin. "Your wins this year were some of the most beautiful things I've ever seen," he says. Christophe had also said as much via text at the time. He has never been shy in his admiration for good racing. Wout still glows with the praise.

"Yours will be, too." Wout is looking forward to seeing it. He might not be there, because he has his own training obligations and other team commitments, but he's determined to watch every race live. Beyond the joy of having Christophe in his life, as *his*, he hates the idea of becoming someone who is always looking backwards before his career is over. He has more races he wants to win. He might not win them. He might not win a race ever again. But he still wants to try. It's still important to try. And who knows, maybe 2026 will be a year for them that will put 2022 to shame.

Christophe nods. "Let's go," he says. "I think I'm ready to go home."

"Me, too," Wout says. He takes Christophe's hand again, and he gives Christophe one last lingering kiss beneath the cycling mural, and then they go.
